## Summary
The definitive academic reference for BIM Project Execution Planning in the US context. It defines BIM, explains why planning is essential, and lays out a structured **five-step procedure** for developing a BIM Project Execution Plan (BEP), plus the categories of information a BEP should contain and who should develop it. This is the source of the widely used "BIM Uses" classification system.

## Key Points & Extracted Knowledge

### Definition of BIM (NBIMS)
- BIM is *"a digital representation of physical and functional characteristics of a facility. A BIM is a shared knowledge resource for information about a facility forming a reliable basis for decisions during its life-cycle; defined as existing from earliest conception to demolition."*
- A basic premise: collaboration by different stakeholders across the life cycle to insert, extract, update or modify information in the BIM.

### Why planning matters
- Well-planned BIM projects yield: increased design quality through analysis cycles, greater prefabrication, improved field efficiency, increased innovation.
- Poorly planned BIM leads to: increased modeling costs, schedule delays from missing information, little added value.
- Teams should not ask "whether to use BIM" but define the **specific implementation** appropriate to the project.

### The Five-Step BIM Project Execution Planning Procedure
1. **Set BIM Goals** — Define the potential value of BIM (project performance goals: reduce schedule, higher field productivity, increase quality, reduce change-order cost, obtain operational data; or capability goals: pilot projects, gaining experience). Goals should be measurable.
2. **Select Model Uses** — Identify and prioritize the specific BIM Uses. A **BIM Use** is "a unique task or procedure on a project which can benefit from the integration of BIM into that process." The guide catalogs **25 common BIM Uses** (e.g. design authoring, 4D modeling, cost estimating, space management, record modeling, energy analysis, coordination, fabrication, site logistics).
3. **Design the BIM Execution Process** — Develop a high-level process map showing sequencing/interaction of primary BIM Uses, then detailed process maps per BIM Use.
4. **Develop Information Exchanges** — Identify information exchanges between participants; define the information content (author and receiver) via an Information Exchange table/worksheet.
5. **Define Supporting Infrastructure** — Delivery structure and contract language, communication procedures, technology infrastructure, and quality-control procedures.

### What a BIM Project Execution Plan should contain
- **Overview information** — reason for creating the plan.
- **Project information** — project number, location, description, critical schedule dates.
- **Key project contacts** — contact info for key personnel.
- **Project goals / BIM objectives** — strategic value and specific uses.
- **Organizational roles and staffing** — who coordinates BIM planning/execution; required staff.
- **BIM process design** — process maps illustrating the execution process.
- **BIM information exchanges** — model elements and level of detail required per BIM Use.
- **BIM and facility data requirements** — owner's requirements.
- **Collaboration procedures** — model management (file structures, permissions), meeting schedules/agendas.
- **Model quality control procedures** — how participants meet defined requirements.
- **Technology infrastructure needs** — hardware, software, network.
- **Model structure** — model structure, file naming, coordinate system, modeling standards.
- **Project deliverables** — deliverables required by the owner.
- **Delivery strategy / contracts** — delivery method (design-build vs design-bid-build) and contract language.

### Who should develop the BEP
- A planning team assembled **early in the project**, with representatives from all primary parties: owner, designers, contractors, engineers, major specialty contractors, facility manager.
- Owner and all primary team members must fully support the process.
- Initial goal-setting meetings need **key decision-makers** from each organization.
- The **lead party** for coordinating/compiling the BEP should be clearly identified; may be the owner, architect, program manager, or construction manager depending on delivery method and timing.
