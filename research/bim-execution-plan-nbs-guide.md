# What is a BIM Execution Plan (BEP)? — NBS Guide

**Source:** NBS (thenbs.com) — "What is a BIM Execution Plan (BEP)?"
**URL:** https://www.thenbs.com/knowledge/what-is-a-bim-execution-plan-bep
**Author:** Richard McPartland (NBS editor)
**Date:** c. 2015–2018 (NBS knowledge article, still current)
**Type:** Industry guide (UK, authoritative AEC source)

## Summary
A concise, authoritative UK-industry explanation of the BIM Execution Plan (BEP, also abbreviated BxP), covering its definition, the pre-contract vs post-contract distinction, the relationship to the Employer's Information Requirements (EIR), and the content a BEP should cover. Rooted in the PAS 1192-2:2013 lineage (predecessor to ISO 19650).

## Key Points & Extracted Knowledge

### Definition
- The BEP is defined in **PAS 1192-2:2013** as *"a plan prepared by the suppliers to explain how the information modelling aspects of a project will be carried out."*
- It is developed **both pre- and post-contract** and is prepared as a **direct response to the Employer's Information Requirements (EIR)**.
- The BEP details the project deliverables stipulated by the contract and the information-exchange requirements detailed in a BIM protocol (e.g. the **CIC BIM Protocol** — a supplementary legal agreement incorporated into construction/professional-services contracts via a simple amendment).

### Pre-contract vs Post-contract BEP
- **Pre-contract (tender stage):** Before a contract is agreed, a prospective supplier develops a BEP to demonstrate their proposed **approach, capability, capacity and competence** to meet the EIR in general terms.
- **Post-contract:** After award, the winning supplier submits a further BEP whose focus is to **confirm the supply chain's capabilities**. It requires far more effort from the now-appointed Principal Designer (CDM 2015 context).

### Relationship to the EIR
- The pre-contract BEP is designed to address the **EIR**, which sets out the information required by an employer at points where key decisions must be made.
- The EIR **supplements but is distinct from the project brief**: the brief defines the nature of the built asset being procured; the EIR defines the *information about* the asset the employer wishes to procure.
- The aim: ensure the design develops in line with the employer's needs and the employer can operate the completed asset effectively.

### Multiple suppliers
- Where several suppliers are appointed, there is likely to be **one main BEP** (responsibility for its production set out in appointment documents). Subsequent BEPs from later appointees must **dovetail with the main BEP**.

### Information Delivery Plans (MIDP / TIDP)
- A **Master Information Delivery Plan (MIDP)** is submitted post-contract, setting out *when* project information is prepared, *who* is responsible, and *what protocols/procedures* are used.
- The MIDP is based on a series of individual **Task Information Delivery Plans (TIDPs)** showing who is responsible for each information deliverable.

### Content the BEP should cover
- **Roles and responsibilities**, including relevant authorities and approval processes.
- A **strategy for key deliverables** and what existing information will be used.
- **Key project milestones** and where they fit in the wider programme.
- **Logistics of collaborative processes** (including modelling) with clear responsibilities.
- A revised **Project Implementation Plan (PIP)**, plus **TIDP** and **MIDP**.
- **Working procedures:** how BIM volumes are managed/maintained, file-naming conventions, construction tolerances, required attribute data.
- A common approach to **annotation, abbreviations and symbols** to avoid ambiguity.
- **Software** to be used, **data formats** for exchange, and other data-management systems.

### Specimen templates
- The article points to specimen pre- and post-contract BEPs on the **CPIC website** (Construction Project Information Committee).

## Notes for further research
- This article reflects the **PAS 1192-2:2013** era; the modern equivalent is **ISO 19650-2** (delivery phase) and ISO 19650-1 (concepts/principles). A follow-up note should map these PAS 1192 concepts onto ISO 19650 terminology (e.g. EIR → "Exchange Information Requirements / Information Requirements", BEP → "BIM Execution Plan" retained, MIDP/TIDP → "Delivery Milestones / Responsibility Matrix").
